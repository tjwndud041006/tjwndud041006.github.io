About Me

I'm an undergraduate at Sungkyunkwan University, double majoring in Computer Education and Computational Statistics, and a research assistant in the SKKU Software Laboratory. My research spans AI in education, trustworthy machine learning, and the evaluation of ML systems.

I study how to tell whether an ML system can be relied on: whether an LLM tutor adapts to the student in front of it, whether a ranking stays fair when AI agents read it, and whether an anomaly detector's error rates hold when its baseline is contaminated. Having trained as a teacher and supported more than a thousand students as a TA, I am most drawn to these questions in education.

The Incident Is Its Own Baseline: Reference Contamination in Forensic Anomaly Calibration

Juyoung Seo, et al.

TL;DR Forensic anomaly detectors must calibrate against the seized exhibit, which already contains the incident. Size-matched controls show this self-contamination lowers detection power and incident discovery, and common reference fixes do not repair it.

DFC Europe 2027Under review

Caution Is Not Discrimination: Measuring Inferential Overreach in LLM-Assisted Digital Forensics

Juyoung Seo, et al.

TL;DR LLMs cite genuine forensic evidence yet assert activity it does not support. We separate the kind of evidence from its relation to the record, and show that generic caution suppresses supported conclusions rather than teaching models to discriminate.
